**Building Together: The Carter Work Project and the Practice of Unity**  
============================================================================================

When you see a half‑built wall or a stack of lumber, do you think only of the work ahead, or do you imagine the hands that will soon turn those materials into a home where a family can eat, rest, and grow?  

## The Human Story  
The Carter Work Project brings people of different ages, faiths, incomes, and nationalities to the same construction site. Since its start in 1984, volunteers have numbered over 108,000 and have helped build, renovate, or repair more than 4,400 homes in 14 countries; later tallies from the project cite 112,031 volunteers, 14 countries, and 4,496 houses. In 2025 the project took place in Austin, Texas, where volunteers built 25 affordable, energy‑efficient homes in the Whisper Valley development. The following year, in May 2026, the project returned to Atlanta and produced 24 new single‑family homes and townhomes in Langston Park. Throughout these builds, former President Jimmy Carter and former First Lady Rosalynn Carter worked side‑by‑side with volunteers, sharing the same tools and tasks.  

## Where Love Is Needed  
The Carters first recognized that a lack of affordable housing leaves families without a stable foundation to grow. Without a safe place to call home, people’s dignity, sense of belonging, and ability to plan for the future are undermined. The Carter Work Project directly addresses this gap by creating decent, affordable housing for families who might otherwise face instability or overcrowding.  

## What Gets in the Way  
Barriers to neighbor‑love often appear as indifference, the belief that housing is someone else’s problem, or the stigma that charity creates a hierarchy of giver and receiver. The Carters observed that Habitat for Humanity “has successfully removed the stigma of charity by substituting it with a sense of partnership.” This shift acknowledges that everyone on the site brings value and that the work is collaborative rather than paternalistic. Fear of difference—whether cultural, economic, or ideological—can also keep people apart, but the shared purpose of building a home creates a concrete space where those differences can be met with curiosity rather than suspicion.  

## Choosing Love in Practice  
Love becomes real when we listen to the stories of those who will live in the homes we help build, when we speak up for policies that expand affordable housing, when we lend our time or skills to a build site, and when we support organizations that treat homeowners as partners. Simple actions—asking a Habitat homeowner what a stable home means to them, sharing accurate information about local housing needs, or volunteering for a day of construction—turn concern into relationship.  

## A Real‑World Witness  
In Austin 2025, volunteers from across the United States and abroad gathered to raise walls, install roofs, and finish interiors for 25 families. The build was not a one‑way gift; homeowners worked alongside volunteers, contributing sweat equity and shaping the design of their future homes. This mutual effort illustrates how shared labor can transform a abstract concern for housing into tangible relationships that honor each person’s dignity and capacity.  

## Questions for Reflection  
1. When I think about housing insecurity, what assumptions do I hold about who is responsible for solving it?  
2. How can I set aside my own comfort to listen to someone whose life experience differs from mine?  
3. What small, regular action could I take this month to support affordable‑housing efforts in my community?  

## A Neighborly Practice  
This week, spend thirty minutes researching a local affordable‑housing initiative or Habitat for Humanity affiliate. Share one thing you learn with a friend, neighbor, or coworker, and ask for their thoughts on how they might get involved.  

## Key Takeaways  
- The Carter Work Project shows how diverse volunteers can build homes together, turning concern into relationship.  
- Affordable housing is a matter of dignity; a stable home enables families to thrive.  
- Partnership—not charity—creates space for mutual respect and shared purpose.  
- Listening to homeowners and advocating for just policies are concrete ways to practice neighbor‑love.  
- Small, informed actions can sustain the spirit of unity beyond a single build event.
